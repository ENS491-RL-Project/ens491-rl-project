## Context

H-5 produced the first composite, `{1,1} unlock_door`, at held-out SR 0.98 — and the mandatory C1
controls showed that **a random policy with initiation masking matched it exactly** (0.98, 19.2 env
steps, byte-identical). At depth 1 on Unlock the hand-written mask fully determines the order, so
success rate alone cannot demonstrate that composition helped. The run summary's own conclusion:

> H-6 (depth 2, `solve_doorkey`) is where the claim gets a harder test, because the mask no longer
> determines the order on its own.

That expectation is **wrong**, and this plan says so up front rather than discovering it in the
results. `solve_doorkey` also has a strict order — unlock the door, then go to the goal — so with
`initiation` declared on its children the strict mask determines that order too, and the
initiation-masked random control will match again. H-6 therefore carries three things, not one:

1. **T5 `{2,1} solve_doorkey`** — the depth-2 result the roadmap asks for, over 3 seeds.
2. **The primary C1 baseline: flat PPO** on the same task, same hyperparameters, 3 seeds — a
   success-rate-vs-env-steps curve, with the composite's env-step cost reported both with and
   without its children's pretraining.
3. **A genuine-choice composite** (§ Part C below) built specifically so that two children are
   *simultaneously valid* and the better one depends on the observation. This is the arm where the
   initiation mask *cannot* substitute for a learned policy, and it is the honest answer to open
   supervisor question **Q8** ("how should the masking contribution be framed in the report?").

**V2 and V3 together retire the plan's single largest schedule risk.** Part C of the predecessor plan
named it: "Did T1/T2 trained on both layouts transfer into DoorKey (F29)? If not, H-6 is blocked until
the primitives are retrained." They did. Nothing in H-6 is blocked on retraining a primitive.

**V7 is a design constraint, not a curiosity.** A composite's child choice is made from one
observation at one instant, by a feedforward policy with no memory. If the signal the choice depends
on is outside the agent's view at that instant, the choice is a coin flip no matter how well the
policy is trained. The genuine-choice task in Part C therefore pins the agent's facing so the signal
is always observable; without that, the experiment would measure partial observability instead of
composition.

### H-6.1 — composite loading, env-step accounting, thread control (3 SP)

Three gaps that H-6's measurements need and that do not exist yet.

**(a) `load_frozen_composite(node, registry, env_id, hook, device)`** — the composite analogue of
`load_frozen_primitive`. A composite's policy shape comes from `OptionEnv`, so it builds a throwaway
`MaskablePPO("MlpPolicy", DummyVecEnv([option_env_factory(...)]))`, attaches
`CompositePolicyAdapter`, then `registry.load_into(..., require_frozen=True)` — which verifies
`spec_hash` and refuses a mismatch (F18). Recursive: children are loaded before the parent, so a
depth-2 node loads its whole subgraph.

**(b) Env-step accounting.** `OptionEnv` already tracks `self.steps` **per episode**; nothing
accumulates across episodes, so a composite's true env-step cost has never been measured —
`unlock_door`'s cost is simply absent from `runs/v2/unlock_door.json`. Add:

- `OptionEnv.total_env_steps` — a cumulative counter, incremented in `step()` alongside `self.steps`,
  never reset by `reset()`
- `OptionEnv.get_total_env_steps()` and a passthrough on `MaskableMonitor`. The passthrough is
  mandatory, for the reason already documented on `action_masks`: Gymnasium 1.x does not forward
  arbitrary attributes through `Wrapper.__getattr__`, so `DummyVecEnv.get_attr` would miss a counter
  living on the inner `OptionEnv`.
- `train_composite` sums `vec.env_method("get_total_env_steps")` after `learn()` and reports
  `env_steps_own`, `env_steps_per_decision`, and `env_steps_children` (summed from the children's
  own `meta`, which the registry already persists).

**(c) `--torch-threads`.** 22 cores, and a `DummyVecEnv` run is almost entirely single-threaded
Python; torch defaults to 16 threads and three concurrent runs would thrash. Each script takes
`--torch-threads` (default 2) and calls `torch.set_num_threads` before building anything, so the 3
seeds of each experiment run as 3 concurrent processes.

**Tests** (`tests/test_h6_accounting.py`):
- the counter equals the sum of per-episode `k` over several episodes, across `reset()`
- `get_total_env_steps` is reachable through `MaskableMonitor` and through `DummyVecEnv.env_method`
- `load_frozen_composite` round-trips a 2-level node and reproduces its selections
- a composite whose child's `spec_hash` changed is refused with `SpecMismatch`

**Gate:** full suite green (190 existing + new), and `env_steps_own` is non-zero and
`>= decisions_trained` on a 200-decision smoke run.

### H-6.3 — `{2,1} solve_doorkey`, 3 seeds, with both masking controls (5 SP)

```
{2,1} solve_doorkey
  children     ({1,1} unlock_door, {0,3} go_to_goal)
  env          MiniGrid-DoorKey-6x6-v0,  no reset hook (the real task, from episode start)
  goal         on_goal_tile()
  initiation   door_locked()
  timeout      360      # >= max child timeout 240 (F13); == DoorKey-6x6 max_steps 360
  max_decisions 8       # mandatory (F2)
  n            2        # derived: 1 + max(1, 0)
```

`goal` is `on_goal_tile()`, not the env's reward or `env_success()`: every node is paid for its own
goal predicate read off the `StateView` (CLAUDE.md, v2 §5.2). On DoorKey the two coincide, which is
what makes the comparison against flat PPO fair.

**Why the timeout is not tight.** A wrong first choice costs a full `go_to_goal` timeout of 120 env
steps, and the successful path measures ~8.6 (`unlock_door`, V2) + ~5.7 (`go_to_goal`, V3) ≈ 15. Two
wrong picks still fit inside 360. So goal-only masking is learnable here without the budget masking
the signal — but the wasted 120 steps are exactly the reward difference the composite must learn
from, and `max_decisions=8` bounds the worst case.

Budget 20,000 decisions, `eval_every` 1,000, `n_eval` 50, seeds **42, 43, 44**, run concurrently.
Smoke run (`--smoke`: 600 decisions, 2 envs, 10 eval episodes) first, as always.

Seed 42 is the canonical T5 and is the one frozen into `runs/v2/2-1_solve_doorkey/`; all three seeds'
reports go to `runs/v2/solve_doorkey.json`. Freezing one of three is stated in the summary, not
implied — the other two are evidence about variance, not separate skills.

**The two mandatory C1 controls**, scored on the same held-out seeds:
- (a) `RandomOverAvailable` over the goal-only mask
- (b) `RandomOverAvailable` over the strict (initiation) mask
- (c) the learned composite, trained and scored under the goal-only mask
- (d) the learned composite, trained and scored under the strict mask

Plus the **divergence count** H-5 introduced: over the evaluation episodes, how many decisions the
strict mask would have withheld, and in how many of those the goal-only-trained policy had already
declined that child. That is the number that shows the policy *learned* the ordering constraint
rather than being handed it, and it is the only part of this arm that survives A2.

**Depth check (A6).** One episode's `Executor.trace` is printed and stored: the stack must reach
depth 3 (`solve_doorkey → unlock_door → get_key`), and `selected_children()` must contain both a
level-1 and a level-0 node. This is the v2 §11 claim "T5 already exercises a mixed-level DAG and a
depth-3 stack", checked rather than asserted.

**Gate:** ≥2 of 3 seeds STABLE and the canonical node FROZEN with a held-out SR; all four arms
reported together; a depth-3 trace stored in `runs/v2/solve_doorkey_trace.json`.

### H-6.4 — primary C1 baseline: flat PPO on DoorKey-6x6, 3 seeds (3 SP)

```
{0,B} flat_doorkey_ppo
  primitive, no children
  env          MiniGrid-DoorKey-6x6-v0,  no reset hook
  goal         on_goal_tile()          # identical to solve_doorkey's goal
  timeout      360                     # identical
  PPO          PPO_DEFAULTS, unchanged: n_steps=256, batch=256, n_epochs=4, lr=3e-4,
               gamma=0.99, gae_lambda=0.95, clip=0.2, ent_coef=0.01
  n_envs       8                       # identical
  criterion    the same StabilityCriterion, the same stopping seeds
```

Everything that could flatter either side is held identical: the same reward
(`1 − 0.9·steps/timeout` on the node's own goal, which on DoorKey is also MiniGrid's native reward),
the same observation pipeline, the same `ColumnPolicy` network, the same stopping rule, the same
held-out seed set, the same evaluation code path (`evaluate_node` through the `Executor` — a
primitive and a composite are scored by one function, v2 §5.3).

Budget **1,500,000** env steps per seed, seeds 42/43/44 concurrent.

**`eval_every` is chosen from a measurement, not guessed.** Each evaluation is 50 single-env episodes
capped at the 360-step timeout, so an evaluation of a policy that fails everything costs up to 18,000
env steps of pure Python inference. At 60 evaluations that is comparable to the training run itself.
The smoke run therefore times one evaluation, and `eval_every` is set so that total evaluation wall
time is ≤ ~40% of training wall time (expected to land at 25,000–50,000). The chosen value and the
measured per-evaluation cost both go into the report, because `eval_every` sets the resolution of the
success-rate-vs-env-steps curve.

**Reported for C1:**

| quantity | composite | flat PPO |
|---|---|---|
| success rate (held-out) | per seed | per seed |
| env steps to STABLE, **own training only** | `env_steps_own` | `steps_trained` |
| env steps to STABLE, **including children's pretraining** | `env_steps_own + env_steps_children` (V9: 480,000 primitive steps + `unlock_door`'s own) | same as above (no children) |
| success rate vs env steps | the evaluation history, x-axis converted from decisions to env steps via the measured `env_steps_per_decision` | the evaluation history directly |

Both framings are printed side by side and neither is called "the" answer. The without-children
number is the fair measure of *this* task's cost once a skill library exists; the with-children number
is the honest total for a learner starting from nothing. v2 §11 already requires both, which is why
the amortization sentence is in the architecture doc.

**Gate:** three curves and a steps-to-STABLE (or an explicit "not stable within 1,500,000") per seed,
written to `runs/v2/flat_baseline.json`, plus the comparison table above. A3 is the stop condition.

# Part C — the genuine-choice task, designed

## What "genuine choice" has to mean

The requirement is sharper than "two children available". Under goal-only masking two children are
almost always available, because availability only asks `not goal(s)`. H-5 showed that is not enough:
the composite still learned a *constant* policy (always `get_key` first), because the correct order
never depended on the state. A constant policy is indistinguishable from a hand-written mask, which is
exactly why control (b) matched.

The design target is therefore:

> There must exist states s₁ and s₂, both reachable at the same decision point, both with ≥2 children
> valid **under the strict initiation mask**, where the optimal child differs between s₁ and s₂ and
> the difference is visible in the 147-dim observation.

Only then is a state-independent policy — learned or masked — provably suboptimal, and the gap is a
measurement of composition rather than of masking.

## The task: two keys, one door, matching colours

Built on DoorKey-6x6 with **reset hooks only — no new environment class.** Hooks are already part of
`spec_hash` (H-5 deviation 3), already reproducible from the env's own RNG, and already the mechanism
H-2 and H-4 use, so the choice task inherits all of that machinery for free.

Per episode, after a normal DoorKey-6x6 reset:

1. **`recolor_door_and_key(colors=("yellow","blue"))`** — draw `C` uniformly from the two colours with
   the env's own `_rand_*`, set the door's colour to `C` and the existing key's colour to `C`. The
   layout, the split position and the door position are untouched.
2. **`add_decoy_key(other_color)`** — place a key of the *other* colour in a random empty cell in the
   agent's region, found with the existing `reachable_cells()` flood fill (which already treats a
   locked door as a wall, so the decoy can never land behind the door) and excluding the agent's own
   cell.
3. **`randomize_agent_pose(anchor=None)`** — existing hook, unchanged.
4. **`face_toward("door")`** — collect the directions in which the door appears in `gen_obs()`, and
   pick one uniformly. Needed because of V7: with a random facing the door is in view in only 54% of
   resets, and a feedforward policy cannot condition on a signal it cannot see. Implemented by
   *checking the observation*, not by hard-coding "face right", so it generalizes to the other
   curriculum envs. Pose diversity is preserved — position is still random, and so is the facing among
   those that keep the door visible.

`two_keys()` is `compose(recolor_door_and_key(), add_decoy_key(), randomize_agent_pose(),
face_toward("door"))`, one hook with one spec string.

**Why colour matching makes the choice real: V5.** `Door.toggle` opens a locked door only if the
carried key's colour matches the door's. The wrong key does not open the door — and because MiniGrid
`pickup` requires an empty hand, and neither key primitive was trained to `drop`, a wrong first pick is
**unrecoverable within the episode**. So the choice separates *success rate*, not merely episode
length. That is deliberate: an efficiency-only gap would be the weaker result.

## The nodes

```
{0,6} get_key_yellow         {0,7} get_key_blue
  primitive                    primitive
  goal        carrying("key","yellow")   |   carrying("key","blue")
  initiation  carrying_nothing()         |   carrying_nothing()
  timeout     120
  env         DoorKey-6x6 + two_keys()
  hook        two_keys()                 # identical to the parent's reset distribution
```

Each colour is present in every episode — exactly one yellow key and one blue key — so each
primitive's goal is always achievable and `SubgoalEnv`'s F7 reset assertion is always satisfiable.

**`randomize_carrying` is deliberately NOT in these hooks**, and this is the one place where the H-4
lesson is intentionally not applied. Randomizing the carried object would teach the primitive to cope
with a full hand (most likely by dropping), which would make a wrong first pick recoverable and blur
the very signal the task is built to produce. The agent is always empty-handed at the only decision
where these children are selected, so the training distribution matches the parent's handoff exactly.
The wrong-key state is logged as an **expected failure**, the same way H-4 treated
"[expected fail] door still shut".

```
{1,2} unlock_matching
  children     ({0,6} get_key_yellow, {0,7} get_key_blue, {0,2} open_door)
  goal         door_open()
  initiation   door_locked() and carrying_nothing()
  env          DoorKey-6x6 + two_keys()
  timeout      240      # >= max child timeout 120 (F13); <= 360
  max_decisions 6
  n            1
```

## Why both masks leave a real choice

Decision 1 — agent empty-handed, door locked:

| child | goal holds? | available (goal-only) | initiation holds? | available (strict) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `get_key_yellow` | no | ✅ | carrying nothing → **yes** | ✅ |
| `get_key_blue` | no | ✅ | carrying nothing → **yes** | ✅ |
| `open_door` | no | ✅ | carrying a key → no | ❌ |

**Two children survive the strict mask, and which one is correct is determined by the door's colour in
the observation.** This is the structural difference from `unlock_door` and `solve_doorkey`, where the
strict mask always left exactly one child.

Decision 2 — the right key in hand:

| child | goal holds? | available (goal-only) | initiation holds? | available (strict) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `get_key_yellow` | yes (if yellow taken) | ❌ | — | ❌ |
| `get_key_blue` | no | ✅ (but unachievable, hand full) | carrying nothing → no | ❌ |
| `open_door` | no | ✅ | carrying a key → **yes** | ✅ |

So the strict mask forces `open_door` at decision 2, as it should. The choice lives entirely at
decision 1, which is what makes the result easy to attribute.

## Predicted numbers, written before the run

Stated so the result can disagree with the plan rather than be explained after the fact.

| arm | predicted SR | reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| random over the goal-only mask | ~0.15–0.35 | must pick the right key (½) then `open_door` from 3 then 2 choices, within 6 decisions |
| **random over the strict mask** | **~0.50** | the mask forces decision 2, so the ceiling is the coin flip at decision 1 |
| learned, goal-only mask | ≥0.90 | the door colour is always visible (V7) and always decisive (V5) |
| learned, strict mask | ≥0.90, converging in fewer decisions | the mask removes the decision-2 error, not the decision-1 choice |

**The gate is non-overlap at 95%**: with N=50 the strict-mask control at 25/50 has a Wilson upper
bound ≈ 0.64, and a learned arm at 46/50 has a Wilson lower bound ≈ 0.80. If the intervals overlap,
A5 is contradicted — the task is not the genuine choice it was designed to be — and that is a stop
and report, not a number to be written up as a success.

## What this task buys the report (Q8)

It makes the masking contribution statable in one honest sentence, which is what Q8 asks for:

> On tasks whose subgoal order is strict — `unlock_door`, `solve_doorkey` — the initiation mask
> encodes the task's ordering constraint, and a random policy under that mask attains the composite's
> success rate; what the learned composite adds there is that it *discovers* the ordering from the
> goal-only mask (measured as the divergence count) and executes it 4.4× more efficiently than random
> choice. On a task where two children are simultaneously valid and the correct one depends on the
> observation, no state-independent mask can substitute: the masked random policy is capped at ~0.50
> while the learned composite reaches ≥0.90.

# Risks accepted, and what would change the plan

| Risk | Signal it is happening | Response |
|---|---|---|
| The initiation-masked random control matches `solve_doorkey` | control (b) ties control (c) | **Expected (A2).** This is why Part C exists; report the tie plainly and let the choice task carry C1 |
| Flat PPO never stabilizes on DoorKey-6x6 within 1.5M | no seed reaches the criterion | A legitimate C1 result, reported as a curve plus "not stable within budget"; **not** extended indefinitely to manufacture a crossing point |
| Flat PPO beats the composite including children's cost | steps-to-STABLE lower than `env_steps_total` | **Stop and report** (A3) — C1 fails in its with-children framing and that must be the supervisor's call, not a framing choice made here |
| `open_door` does not transfer into the two-key env | F29 check < 0.90 | Train `{0,8} open_door_tk` on the wider mix; branch already specified in H-6.6, no stop |
| The two key primitives learn to grab whichever key is nearer rather than the right colour | one of them stabilizes but the other does not, or SR ≈ 0.5 for both | The goal predicate is colour-specific, so SR ≈ 0.5 means the colour channel is not being used; report and investigate before training the parent |
| The choice composite does not separate from the masked control | overlapping Wilson intervals | **Stop and report** (A5) |
| Evaluation dominates wall time on the 1.5M baseline | smoke-run evaluation timing says >40% | Raise `eval_every` (coarser curve, same criterion), never reduce `n_eval` below 50 — that would change the stopping rule |
| A depth-3 stack never forms | the trace stays at depth ≤ 2 | **Stop and report** (A6) |
| Retraining `unlock_door` diverges from H-5 | stable at ≠ 6,000 decisions | Report as a deviation and demote the env-step figure to "same-seed replay"; no stop |

**CLAUDE.md stop-and-report conditions remain in force verbatim** and override everything above:
lateral activations unreachable in a custom policy's forward pass, broken gradient flow through
laterals, VecEnv incompatible with lateral sharing, and `OptionEnv` not vectorizable because frozen
children hold per-env state.
